# Prospect Hollow demo

Crystal Cascade opens on an English/French landing page explaining the jewel puzzles and the village. Enter the village, choose a construction, then click the mine entrance or its label to play the next unfinished level. Results put two buttons on one row above the coin recap: Continue mining with a pickaxe for the next unfinished level, and Back to village with a house. Welcome, Village, and Mine remain available in the header on desktop and phones; the Museum tab appears once it is built. Mine starts the next unfinished puzzle and stays selected while playing; clicking it during a run keeps that run. After the campaign is complete it opens the museum, where completed levels live. There is no separate level-selection page.

**The village opens full screen.** Compact coin and builder-hammer counters, a book button for neighbor stories and raid news, and the close control stay over the scene. An eye button beside the book toggles all floating labels, including the mine label. Labels start on; the preference is saved separately from progress and survives mine visits and browser sessions. Hidden labels do not disable building or parcel interaction and toggling them does not rebuild the 3D scene. The book opens a native popup without leaving full screen, with live population/resident/visitor counts, coin savings, water and food capacity versus demand, happiness, saloon rate and stored earnings, construction counts, and builder-hammer inventory above the village story. Persistent camera/help text is removed. “Available plots” shows only unlocked, unfinished tiers affordable with the current wallet. Owning a builder hammer includes every otherwise eligible purchase; work already in progress and locked or maximum tiers stay out of the list. Parcel-list entries directly spend the displayed coins, or one builder hammer when coins are insufficient, without opening a building card or collecting resources. Coin prices remain visible beside hammer actions. Ready construction comes first, followed by purchases from cheapest to most expensive. Finishing or buying keeps the list open so several actions can be performed in succession. The bottom progress panel is a persistent toggle: open initially on desktop and closed initially on small screens, retaining its state through building interactions. In the town, tapping a building or its action icon collects its ready resource or finishes construction. White labels identify constructed buildings; blue labels identify available builds/upgrades; green labels identify collection and completion actions. Selection adds an outline without changing these colors. A light-green glow surrounds the base and lower walls of buildings with immediately eligible coin purchases, fading by roughly 45% of building height. Two simple surfaces per plot are cached with the 3D town and use normal depth testing, so neighboring buildings hide the glow. The SVG fallback draws a green ground halo behind each building. Hammer-only work is excluded. Ready actions use small icons in front of the buildings: coins for saloon earnings, TNT for the blacksmith, a hammer for construction waiting to be finished, and a warning bell during raids for a completed level-4 town square. Unaffordable empty plots retain their barriers and can be inspected directly, with their name tags hidden. Hammer inventory changes purchase eligibility, never parcel geometry. Native dialogs pause village activity and audio, contain keyboard focus, support Escape, and restore focus on closing.

**A small village, with automatic basic needs.** Eleven initial plots, including the town square, start bare. Choose any original plot for free first materials, and fund multiple constructions when you can afford them. Every normal puzzle completion advances all active projects once. After the required puzzle, scaffolding remains and the project becomes ready. Tap each building to finish it; only then do its new services, income, population, replay access, and storage benefits activate. Readiness survives reloads and later mine runs. A one-time helper explains the first ready building. One builder hammer pays for an unlocked building or improvement in full and completes it instantly; it cannot finish a project already funded with coins. Starting or opening work plays a one-second assembly animation with hammer taps and a chime. Supporting buildings finish at **level 3**; the town square, sheriff, bank, saloon and blacksmith finish at **level 5**, including the initial build; their existing services remain active during improvements. Wooden scaffolding, platforms, diagonal braces, a ladder, and material stacks surround the existing 3D building during upgrades. New benefits activate only when work finishes.

Upgrading the original **home to level 2** unlocks House II; each extra house must finish level 2 to reveal the next; the original **well to level 2** unlocks one extra well; the original **farm to level 2** unlocks Farm II, then finishing Farm II’s level 2 upgrade reveals Farm III. There are **22 Frontier plots in total**, with 3 levels for supporting buildings and 5 for the town square, sheriff, bank, saloon and blacksmith. All upgrades are available without a mine-level or completed-puzzle lock. Coins and builder hammers follow the same building prerequisites. Locked plots remain prairie in 3D and are excluded from the purchase list. Extra plots use the same cost, duration, and service progression as their original type. Resident population is the minimum of housing, water, and food capacity. Stables support 2, 4 and 10 visitors at levels 1–3; the museum supports 0, 2 and 8. Visitors use food and water left after residents, without needing houses. The fully upgraded town supports **40 residents and 18 visitors**. There is no food depletion or manual supply chore.

The catalog is in `src/data/town.js`. Basic wells, farms, and houses open immediately; all improvements and larger buildings finish after one normal puzzle. A ready project persists until the player opens it. The first chosen building is still free. Services remain active while upgrading.

**A lived-in village.** Completed building levels add up to a development score. Narrow dirt tracks connect the bare plots from the start; wooden boardwalks arrive at 12 points and street lamps at 24. Lots are spaced seven to eight world units apart, with open yards and a broad ring of prairie before the surrounding hills. Buildings, entrance paths, residents, mounted visitors and raids share the expanded layout. Residents walk and work, customers enter the saloon, stay inside briefly, and leave again; stables add articulated mounted travelers following the streets. Horses have jointed legs, moving heads and tails, reins, saddles, and seated riders. Matching actor parts use GPU instancing to reduce draw calls as the cast grows. Drag to rotate, Shift-drag or middle-drag to pan, and scroll to zoom. Two-finger touch gestures pan and pinch to zoom. Camera clearance and Reset view remain available.

**Town square and happiness.** A compact fountain sits at the center from the first build, gains a raised basin at level 3, and is surrounded by benches, raised flower beds, and four lantern posts at level 5. Static opaque water and shared geometry add no animation loop or simulation. The 3D and SVG views show the same design. Basic food/water coverage for all desired residents and visitors contributes up to 40 happiness points; each square level adds 8 and each saloon level adds 2; the museum contributes 2, 4 or 10 at levels 1–3. Happiness is capped at 100 and shown in a gauge beneath the map; tapping it opens the square. Food and water never deplete.

**Collection cooldown.** Each successful saloon or blacksmith collection starts its own saved 30-second cooldown. Production and accumulation continue under their usual storage limits. During the cooldown, direct taps open the building card and show the remaining seconds; the ready-resource icon returns when the cooldown ends. Empty or failed collections do not start a cooldown.

**Saloon income.** Store **floor(2.25 × saloon level × total population × (1 + 1.25 × happiness / 100)) coins per hour**. Residents and visitors both count. Earnings stay inside the saloon, with capacity for **8 hours at its current rate**; continued online accrual respects this cap too. Tap the saloon to transfer its stored coins into the wallet. Its light green label shows what can be collected. The first tap collects with an arcade coin burst and up to five ascending coin chimes; a later tap opens the building details. Collection is credited once, independently of the animation. Reduced motion uses a static receipt. The building card shows stored earnings without collecting; return to the town and tap the building or coin icon to collect. Visiting the village, completing a mine run, and improving buildings only accrue earnings; they never collect them. Settle at the old rate before benefits change. Fractional earnings survive saves; repeated timestamps and clock rollback cannot duplicate earnings. Already stored coins survive a lower income rate. This browser clock is a demo mechanism; authoritative time belongs in the Symfony follow-up.

**Mounted bandit raids.** Once residents move in, the first raid is scheduled after a random gap of 3–7 normal puzzle completions. Later raids use a fresh saved gap of 3–7 completions in Frontier or 6–14 in River & Rail; continuous play does not advance the raid counter. Village visits and reloads do not reroll the schedule. There are no timed/offline attacks. Gangs have **2 riders below 16 development points, 4 from 16, 6 from 30, 8 from 50, and 10 from 70**. For each service, bank or sheriff, a completed level covers half the risk from two riders, capped at half the gang’s total risk. Both need the appropriate level for full protection; upgrades retain existing protection until finished.

The initial outcome and wallet deduction are saved **before** the animation: riders arrive from the ridge, fire stylized warning shots, approach the mine and a completed building, and leave with sacks if they stole coins. Sheriff patrols ride out to scare them away. If protection is insufficient, the protected share retreats early while the remaining bandits finish the robbery. Loss is the minimum of 30 coins, `ceil(5 × riders × (1 - combinedProtection))`, 10% of the wallet rounded down, and the amount above a **50-coin reserve**; thus never more than 30 coins. Buildings, inventory and construction progress are never damaged. Save the gang, sheriff and bank levels, targets, run counter, receipt ID, outcome, loss, and presentation status together. Reloading an unfinished presentation resumes the saved story without another deduction. Finishing a ready sheriff or bank before the animation ends updates the current raid’s protection and immediately refunds only the reduction in its saved loss. Their finish buttons remain visible during the raid, and new patrols join without restarting it. Completed raids and replays cannot receive further refunds. At town square level 4 or 5, tapping the square, its bell icon, or the raid bell button halves the remaining loss (rounded down), refunding the difference immediately. The bell works once per unseen raid with a positive loss; its use persists across reloads and combines with new sheriff/bank protection. Fully upgraded sheriff and bank together prevent all losses even against ten riders. “Skip animation” settles the current outcome. Incomplete raid receipts are discarded.

A newly seen fully protected raid ends with a green-and-gold “Village defended!” arcade notice, a sheriff badge, rising stars and a short victory chime. The shared raid notice preserves the loss receipt for stolen coins, dismisses after seven seconds, and uses a static display with reduced motion. Replaying a seen raid does not repeat either receipt or the victory chime. A harmless visit caused by an empty wallet is not treated as a successful defense.

Raids have an approximately 21-second active timeline, pause with Settings, the museum, or a hidden tab, and clear their actors afterward. Reduced motion and unavailable WebGL show the saved outcome immediately with a Continue button. The expanded SVG fallback remains selectable and depicts upgrades/scaffolding.

**The museum.** Build and select the museum, then open its collection inside the village. Normal replay cards show completed levels, stars, best score, and best active time, grouped by chapter. Normal replays retain completion income, chests, and construction progress. The completed museum is required at the game action boundary, including immediate replay from results. Until then the mine always opens the next unfinished puzzle. After the last campaign level, the mine opens the museum (or selects its plot if it still needs building).

Continuous play offers every unlocked level, including the next unfinished one. It keeps the board playable after obstacles and relic objectives are cleared, without a victory interruption. Scores are stored separately from normal campaign records; it grants no stars, chests, level unlocks, builder hammers, or construction steps. Earn one coin per ten collected jewels plus the chapter depth bonus, capped at **25 coins per level across all continuous visits**. Reopening the mode or reloading cannot refresh that allowance. Once capped, play and personal-best scores continue without more currency. Earnings and personal bests save after scoring resolutions; the board itself is not resumed after reload. Normal puzzle bonuses use the real inventory. Return to the village/museum whenever desired. This lifetime cap is deliberately simple for the demo, not a daily system.

**Chests and roulette.** Every completed normal puzzle earns at least one chest; score and speed qualifications can give two. Each chest contains one reward. When all items fit, automatic rolls remain 70% puzzle powers, 20% coins and 10% builder hammers; a persisted counter guarantees a hammer within ten rolls while hammer storage has room. Automatic prizes skip full items and reserve room for unopened rewards. Completion saves pending receipts and fallback awards. Tap the moving roulette itself to claim the symbol nearest its selection line. Each chest opening shuffles the reward order. The sharp, steady reel gives each item approximately 310.5 ms and shows the full selection twice before stopping automatically after 4.347 seconds, a 5% speed increase. Tapping the prize continues. Skip, exit, next-run and reload paths claim unopened fallback rewards once. Each choice removes its receipt in the same local save as the inventory update. A cheerful original chime replaces the bomb explosion. See the [casual progression pass](casual-progression.md) for chapter gifts, shorter forge cycles and village life.

Inventory starts at **zero**, with an initial limit of **3 of each puzzle bonus**, raised to 5, 8, 12, 16, then 20 by completed armory tiers. A full slot converts an incoming bonus into 10 coins. Inventory grants and chest settlement share the same bounded path. The armory panel lists each quantity/limit, and the puzzle toolbar shows the limit. An armory ready after a victory keeps its old capacity until the player taps to finish construction. Already-settled overflow rewards are not reversed.

**Builder hammers are distinct from the TNT.** Up to five are stored separately, independent of armory tiers. On an unlocked plot with no active construction, one builder hammer replaces the full coin cost and instantly completes the next building level, including its benefits. It cannot finish construction already in progress. It never advances other plots, awards mining income, or changes campaign records. Locked plots, stale commands, fully upgraded buildings, and empty stock cannot spend. Excess hammer rewards also convert into 10 coins each. There are no free starting builder hammers; testing fixtures may seed earned stock.

**Shop.** Each completed normal mine run refreshes 1, 2 and 5 distinct puzzle-power offers at shop levels 1–3. Each offer is one item; upgrades add a shelf without restocking sold offers. Clear Row/Shuffle cost 60 coins, other puzzle powers 90. Building, upgrade and shop prices are all 50% above the original catalog, rounded up to whole coins; free first materials stay free and funded construction is not charged again. Builder hammers are available only in mine bonus chests. Buying validates the completed shop, current stock generation, unsold status, funds, and armory capacity before debiting. Stock and purchases persist; abandoning, reloading and continuous play never reroll the shop.

**Tour, fullscreen, and mine appearance.** New villages show a seven-part illustrated introduction, with a dismiss action and a route to the first building; the tour can be reopened from the village info icon. Food/water/population controls open the relevant building. Fullscreen expands the map to the mobile viewport with an exit icon, Escape, scroll restoration and resize handling. Completed chapter boundaries add ten cumulative cosmetic mine upgrades at no cost, in both 3D and SVG. The mining (i) guide pauses the clock and introduces each obstacle once; its illustrations match the new seal/chain/exit art and hit badges.

**Audio.** `TownSoundscape` plays local recordings: a complete, streamed acoustic performance, a 24-second birdsong loop, a 35-second distant crowd loop when residents or visitors are present, and occasional construction, mining, horse, hoof and raid effects. All assets are 44.1 kHz / 192 kbps MP3; ambience loop seams are crossfaded offline. Music is streamed to keep mobile decoded memory bounded; short effects and ambience are fetched/decoded during each village visit, only when eligible and audible. Returning reuses the browser’s asset cache. Activity cues are spaced 11–20 seconds apart. Starting a paid or hammer-funded build plays the recorded timber taps once, even when the building completes instantly. Raids suppress ordinary ambience, duck music and play recorded hooves or warning shots on visible phases. The effects bus is 10% quieter overall. Camera distance smoothly scales it from 30% at the widest view to 100% at the closest; music volume is independent. Camera buttons, wheel, pinch and reset all report actual distance through `TownScene`. The SVG fallback uses a middle distance. Dialogs, settings, hidden tabs and leaving the village stop timers and voices. Within a village visit, music resumes at its paused position; pending sample loads cannot restart stale sounds. Failed samples are isolated with a retry cooldown, autoplay denial retries on a gesture, and app teardown or progress reset aborts requests and releases media/context resources. Mine entry synchronously disposes the village soundscape, disconnects its outputs, aborts pending requests, releases decoded buffers and closes its audio context. Village audio is recreated only when the village becomes visible again; gestures in normal or continuous gem play cannot recreate it. Sources, licenses and processing details are in [audio credits](../public/sound/village/credits.html), also linked from Settings. Regenerate assets with `FFMPEG=/path/to/ffmpeg node scripts/prepare-village-audio.mjs`.

**Economy tuning.** Saloon income is `floor(2.25 × saloon level × population × (1 + 1.25 × happiness / 100))` coins/hour. New coin chests pay 500 / 1,000 / 1,500 in the first three chapters, then add 250 per chapter up to 4,000. Previously earned unopened chests keep the old chapter table; see the [leisure and economy update](leisure-and-economy.md). Normal completion pays **one coin per collected gem, plus ten coins per bonus gem remaining on the settled board**, earned combo/simultaneous-match rewards, and the chapter depth bonus. The results screen counts up the saved total with a coin burst and shows both quantities; reduced motion displays the final amount immediately. All building and shop coin prices are increased by 50%; free first materials are unchanged. Chest coins and overflow are additional income shown in their reward receipts. Larger buildings and improvements now take one normal puzzle; basic buildings open immediately. Continuous mode has its separate small allowance so it cannot indefinitely fund the village. Keep decisions to one clear action, without maintenance bills, resource ratios, workers, or real-time construction timers.

**3D presentation.** `TownDiorama.js` uses original Three.js geometry for timber buildings, staged construction, animated articulated residents, horses, and a mine cart. The museum has a porch and crystal displays; armory upgrades add supply crates and storage. A separate continuous rolling prairie has winding tracks, branched trees, hills, sparse grass, rocks, cacti, and fog. The landscape is built once per scene. The overview frames the actual lots for desktop and portrait views; zoom, rotate and reset remain available. The SVG fallback shares the same plot and path layout. Starting construction rebuilds town objects, preserving the camera. Static geometry uses indexed vertex-color batches, while animated parts share instanced geometry and clothing colors. The static view is cached with its depth buffer, so people remain correctly occluded by buildings without redrawing the entire prairie every frame. Camera/building/selection changes and resizing refresh the cache. Pointer camera changes are coalesced per frame; shadows update only when needed. Ambient animation can run up to 60 fps and pixel ratio remains capped at 1.5. The animation clock advances while moving the camera; the scheduled camera frame draws the updated pose without a second draw. Windmill rotors and the mine cart share the animated pass. Reduced motion stops ambient animation while manual camera controls remain usable.

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, or use the accessible camera buttons. With the canvas focused, arrows rotate, plus/minus zoom, and Home resets the view. The camera remains above terrain. Labels follow the view and hide when they overlap or leave the frame; affordable eligible plots also appear in the available-purchases list. Dragging and pinching do not select plots or enter the mine. Museum dialogs and Settings pause the town. Renderers stay outside Vue’s reactive graph and retain one village scene through mine visits, with animation paused while hidden. Village audio is disposed on departure and recreated on return. Teardown and progress reset dispose controls, geometry, materials, textures, observers, and WebGL contexts. Hidden construction updates are deferred until returning; context restoration invalidates the cached frame. The SVG fallback supports the same construction, museum, armory, and mining actions when WebGL is unavailable. The landing illustration reuses original SVG building art and loads no 3D renderer.

Mining coin payouts scale with the chapter number: chapter 1 pays 1×, chapter 2 pays 2×, chapter 3 pays 3×, through 12× in chapter 12. The bonus applies to the gem, leftover bonus and combo subtotal, rounded down once and shown separately in the results recap. Replays use the chapter being played. Continuous mining uses the same depth bonus on its gem earnings and retains its existing per-level coin cap.

**Persistence and accounting.** `localProfile` is the replaceable storage boundary; there is no backend or account requirement. The profile stays under `crystal-cascade-profile-v3` with normal/continuous records, wallet, bounded powers, builder hammers, building stages, projects, events, and run markers. Earlier profile generations are excluded by the release reset. Current construction and raid receipts are validated on load. Saved over-cap inventory is converted once into 10 coins per excess item, with a visible explanation; saving/reloading retains the normalized quantities and coin balance. Normal victory saves campaign progress, construction readiness, pending chest receipts, shop refresh and mining payout together. Claiming a roulette saves its selected award and removes the pending receipt atomically. Continuous receipts validate the active run and track credited earnings independently. Duplicate or stale settlement cannot pay twice.

**Release reset.** This release starts a new local progress generation at `crystal-cascade-profile-v3`. Earlier v1/v2 profiles are not imported. Campaign and continuous records, inventories, coins, buildings, construction, rewards, raids, income and tutorials all start from zero. New progress survives reloads and later deployments using the same key. Old open tabs write to the old key and cannot restore previous progress. This is a device-local game; no remote accounts exist to reset.

**Language and tester reset.** All landing, town, museum, armory, puzzle, chest, Settings, sign, and accessibility text follows the first supported browser preference (French or English, otherwise English). A future account setting can use the same locale boundary. Settings → Reset progress for testing asks for confirmation and clears normal/continuous records, allowance usage, powers, hammers, money, buildings, work, raid receipts/counters, and income checkpoints. Cancellation preserves everything. The confirmed reset returns to the bare village and persists across reload.

Existing engine, fusion, input, active-time, reward, camera-clearance, and localization regressions remain in place. Entering the mine from the town, navigation or museum prompts for confirmation when any construction or modernization is ready for its final tap. “Back to town” returns to the buildings; “Continue anyway” starts the chosen level and mode without completing projects. Work still needing puzzles does not trigger the reminder, and continuing an existing mine session does not interrupt it.

The mine recap adds settled chest coin prizes and overflow conversions to the displayed town-coin total, with a separate “Bonus chest coins” row. Mining depth multipliers apply only to mining earnings. Showing the recap never awards coins a second time.

Additional houses, farms and wells cost progressively more at each level: the second plot costs 1.5× the original, the third 2×, and the fourth house 2.5× (rounded up to whole coins). Previously paid construction and legacy upgrade refunds keep their original prices.

**Console testing resources.** In the browser developer console, run
`prospectDebug.grant({ coins: 100000, hammers: 5 })` (or simply `prospectDebug.grant()`).
The command adds coins, tops up builder hammers up to their normal cap of five, saves
immediately, and returns the resulting balances. It is available in development and
built previews. Invalid amounts are rejected; failed saves restore the previous balances.

### Mine cliff and watermill

The mine portal opens into a continuous solid rock shoulder joined to the hill.
Building the rail depot opens a tunnel through that shoulder for the railway. The surrounding prairie, river,
and later districts keep their existing layout.

The Frontier watermill has its own upstream west-bank parcel at `(21.5, -12)`,
separate from the industrial mill, river port, future lots and boat channel.
It unlocks after Farm level 1 and uses the normal three-stage construction and
three-stage modernization lifecycle in every subsequent era. It is a visual
landmark, with no new income or capacity rule. A curved channel is cut into the
riverbank at river level, with sloped earth banks and the wheel dipping into it. Its wheel stays animated through
the existing cached moving-part lifecycle. Existing saves receive an empty plot.

`src/data/watermill.js` shares style and capability-based appearances between
offers, Three.js and SVG, so future eras inherit supported visuals. Layout
regressions cover the full mill footprint at every tier and era, the rail
cutting, access route, saved construction and older saves.

The camera can orbit down to a near-horizontal view. Collision protection samples
only the ground and rock immediately around the camera, including the mine
shoulder; an obscured distant parcel never forces the view overhead. Small
collision corrections preserve the viewing direction and zoom distance where
possible, without repeatedly lifting an already-clear camera.
